A market inefficiency exists when asset prices temporarily or structurally deviate from their true fair value due to institutional constraints, behavioral biases, or structural market plumbing. Finding an edge requires identifying who is on the losing side of the trade and why their structural constraints force them to trade sub-optimally.
The 4 Major Classes of Market Inefficiencies
1. Structural & Regulatory Mandate Constraints
Large institutions (pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, passive ETFs) manage trillions of dollars under strict regulatory and contractual rules. They are price-insensitive executioners who must buy or sell regardless of price.
 * Index Rebalancing (Additions & Deletions): When an asset is added to or removed from the S&P 500 or MSCI benchmarks, passive funds must mechanically purchase or dump millions of shares on specific calendar dates.
 * Credit Rating & Mandate Cliffs: Institutional bond and equity funds often have mandates prohibiting them from holding assets below a specific credit rating (e.g., investment-grade vs. high-yield). When an asset is downgraded from BBB- to BB+, institutions are forced to liquidate simultaneously, creating artificial fire-sale discounts.
 * Mutual Fund Window Dressing & Month-End Rebalancing: At quarter-end, asset managers sell underperforming assets and buy winners to display favorable holdings in client reports, creating predictable short-term momentum and subsequent mean-reversion.
2. Derivative Hedging & Market Maker Positioning
Market makers in options and futures strive to stay delta-neutral. Their automated hedging requirements create massive, predictable liquidity flows in the underlying spot markets.
 * Gamma Imbalances (Gamma Squeezes & Pins):
   * Positive Gamma Regime: When dealers are net long options gamma, they buy when the underlying drops and sell when it rises, suppressing market volatility and causing mean-reversion.
   * Negative Gamma Regime: When dealers are net short gamma, they must buy as price rises and sell as price falls to maintain neutrality. This amplifies directional breakouts and volatility cascades.
 * Volatility Risk Premium (VRP) & Roll Yield: Implied volatility systematically trades higher than realized volatility because market participants overpay for downside insurance. Quant funds harvest this spread via delta-hedged short volatility strategies or harvesting the contango roll yield in VIX futures.
3. Market Microstructure & Order Flow Frictions
Microstructure inefficiencies live in the sub-second to intraday timeframe and stem from order routing, exchange latency, and bid-ask inventory imbalances.
 * Order Book Imbalances (Queue Priority & Toxicity): Skews in limit order book depth (bid volume vs. ask volume) provide short-term predictive signals on immediate price drift.
 * Lead-Lag Relationships Across Fragmented Markets: Identical or highly correlated assets traded on multiple venues (e.g., Spot vs. Futures, or ADRs vs. primary exchange listings) rarely move simultaneously down to the millisecond. Faster algorithms capture the latency spread.
 * Retail Flow Imbalance: Retail market orders are largely uninformed and prone to herding. Market makers capture the bid-ask spread on this "uninformed" order flow while shading quotes away from toxic institutional orders.
4. Behavioral Biases & Information Underreaction
Human psychology and institutional latency prevent prices from adjusting instantaneously to new information.
 * Post-Earnings Announcement Drift (PEAD): Large institutional investors cannot deploy hundreds of millions of dollars into a stock on the day of an earnings surprise without blowing out the spread. Their gradual accumulation creates a persistent multi-week or multi-month drift in the direction of the surprise.
 * The Disposition Effect & Anchoring: Traders tend to sell winning positions too early to lock in psychological satisfaction and hold losing positions too long to avoid acknowledging failure. This causes overhead supply resistance at major prior price peaks.

Every 15 minutes (or on every bar close), the system calculates three regime variables:

 * Trend Strength (\text{ADX}_{14}): Values above 25 confirm strong directional momentum.
 * Volatility Dispersion (\text{ATR}_{20} Percentile): Determines if the market is compressing or expanding.
Layer 3: Dynamic Alpha Routing
Based on the Layer 2 classification, capital weights (w_i) are routed to the corresponding sub-strategy:
 * If Regime = Trend (H > 0.55, \text{ADX} > 25):
   * Active Sub-Model: Trend Momentum / Donchian Breakouts (w_{\text{trend}} = 1.0).
   * Exit Strategy: Structural trailing stops behind prior swing pivots to capture asymmetric fat-tail runs (+3R \text{ to } +8R).
 * If Regime = Range (H < 0.45, \text{ADX} < 20):
   * Active Sub-Model: Kalman Filter Cointegration / Statistical Arbitrage (w_{\text{range}} = 1.0).
   * Exit Strategy: Mean reversion target at the dynamic spread mean (Z = 0.0) with a hard time-stop governed by the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck half-life (\lambda).
 * If Regime = High-Vol Crisis / Ambiguous (H \approx 0.50, ATR Spike > 90\text{th} percentile):
   * Action: All sub-strategy weights drop to 0.0; capital shifts to 100\% cash preservation.
Layer 4: Central Risk & Volatility Sizing Engine
The sub-models only generate direction (+1 or -1) and structural invalidation levels. Layer 4 independently determines exact lot sizing:

1. The Regime Lag & Hysteresis Trap
 * The Problem: The Hurst Exponent (H), \text{ADX}_{14}, and \text{ATR}_{20} are backward-looking rolling calculations. During sudden macro events, market structure breaks in milliseconds, but your rolling metrics take 14 to 50 bars to reflect the change.
 * The Failure: The model continues running mean-reversion while a runaway trend is already tearing through stop-losses. By the time H > 0.55 and \text{ADX} > 25 register, the trend is exhausted, prompting the system to buy the top of the move.
2. The Passive Order "Adverse Selection" Paradox
 * The Problem: Placing passive limit orders at the micro-price (P_{\text{micro}}) sounds optimal for fee minimization, but it creates severe adverse selection:
   * When your signal is correct (price explodes in your direction), your passive order is left behind and never fills (100% missed alpha).
   * When your signal is wrong (a large market participant dumps heavy volume through the book), your order gets filled instantly (100% toxic fills).
 * The Failure: Your live win rate and average payoff collapse relative to backtests because you only participate in losing trades.
3. Linear Correlation Collapse in Fat-Tail Crises
 * The Problem: The portfolio filter assumes an asset correlation cap of r < 0.30 based on rolling Pearson correlation.
 * The Failure: Pearson correlation measures linear relationships under Gaussian (normal) distributions. During liquidity shocks (e.g., market crashes, geopolitical surprises), cross-asset correlations collapse to \approx 1.0 (tail dependence). A portfolio holding 3 "uncorrelated" long positions suddenly acts as a single, levered 3.0R directional bet.
4. Retail Broker Microstructure Asymmetries (MT5 B-Book Realities)
 * The Problem: The model treats MT5 as a Direct Market Access (DMA) venue. Most retail MT5 brokers run B-Book dealer desks equipped with Virtual Dealer Plugins.
 * The Failure: Brokers pass negative slippage directly to the trader while capping positive slippage. Stop-losses are filled with artificial execution delays (50ms–300ms) during volatile spikes, turning an expected -1.0R loss into a realized -1.8R loss.
5. Stop-Loss Clustering & Liquidity Sweeps
 * The Problem: Placing hard stops at obvious technical swing points exposes your orders to institutional liquidity sweeps. High-frequency algorithms routinely drive price just past obvious swing highs/lows to trigger retail stop clusters before reversing.

The Square-Root Law of Market Impact
Empirical microstructure research (Barra, CFM, Almgren) proves that market impact scales with the square root of order size relative to daily volume:
 * Y: Dimensionless constant characteristic of the market (Y \approx 0.5 \text{ to } 0.7).
 * \sigma: Daily price volatility (standard deviation of returns).
 * Q: Order size (shares/contracts/coins).
 * V_{\text{ADV}}: Average Daily Volume.
The Almgren-Chriss Framework (Temporary vs. Permanent Impact)
For execution algorithms (TWAP/VWAP/POV) liquidating or accumulating inventory over time:
 * Permanent Impact (I_{\text{perm}}): The price change caused by information leakage that remains in the market after the order finishes executing.
 * Temporary Impact (I_{\text{temp}}): Instantaneous price pressure caused by liquidity consumption at instantaneous trading rate v_t, which decays once trading ceases.

        # Dynamic spread sensitivity parameters
        kappa_vol: float = 0.5,   # Sensitivity of spread to relative ATR expansion
        kappa_liq: float = 0.5,   # Sensitivity of spread to volume depletion
        
        # Explicit fee schedules (US Equity Tiered Default)
        comm_per_share: float = 0.0035,
        min_commission: float = 1.00,
        sec_fee_rate: float = 0.0000278,   # Applied on sell notional
        finra_taf_per_share: float = 0.000166, # Applied on sell shares
        exchange_fee_per_share: float = 0.0030 # Taker fee
    ):
        self.gamma = gamma
        self.eta = eta
        self.alpha = alpha
        self.kappa_vol = kappa_vol
        self.kappa_liq = kappa_liq
        self.comm_per_share = comm_per_share
        self.min_commission = min_commission
        self.sec_fee_rate = sec_fee_rate
        self.finra_taf_per_share = finra_taf_per_share
        self.exchange_fee_per_share = exchange_fee_per_share

    def compute_dynamic_spread(self, order: OrderContext, market: MarketContext) -> float:
        """
        Calculates dynamic dollar spread:
        S_t = S_base * (1 + kappa_vol * (ATR_t / ATR_base) + kappa_liq * (Vol_base / Vol_t)) * M_session
        """
        vol_ratio = market.current_atr / max(market.baseline_atr, 1e-6)
        liq_ratio = market.avg_bar_volume / max(market.current_volume, 1e-6)

        dynamic_spread = (
            order.base_spread
            * (1.0 + self.kappa_vol * (vol_ratio - 1.0) + self.kappa_liq * (liq_ratio - 1.0))
            * market.session_multiplier
        )
        return max(dynamic_spread, order.base_spread * 0.5)

    def compute_market_impact(self, order: OrderContext, market: MarketContext) -> tuple[float, float]:
        """
        Calculates Almgren-Chriss permanent and temporary price impact per share.
        
        Permanent Impact: I_perm = gamma * sigma_daily * P_0 * (Q / V_ADV)
        Temporary Impact: I_temp = eta * sigma_daily * P_0 * (v / V_ADV)^alpha
        """
        participation_ratio = order.order_size / max(market.adv, 1.0)
        
        # Trading rate v = Q / T
        trading_rate = (order.order_size / max(order.duration_bars, 1)) / max(market.adv, 1.0)

        # Permanent price shift (dollar per share shift in equilibrium price)
        perm_impact = self.gamma * market.daily_volatility * market.arrival_price * participation_ratio

        # Temporary price shift (instantaneous liquidity depletion)
        temp_impact = (
            self.eta
            * market.daily_volatility
            * market.arrival_price
            * (trading_rate ** self.alpha)
        )
        return perm_impact, temp_impact

    def compute_explicit_costs(self, order: OrderContext, exec_notional: float) -> float:
        """Calculates broker commissions, SEC/FINRA regulatory fees, and exchange costs."""
        # Broker Commission
        comm = max(self.min_commission, order.order_size * self.comm_per_share)
        
        # Exchange routing fees
        exchange_fee = order.order_size * self.exchange_fee_per_share

        # Regulatory Fees (applied on sell legs)
        reg_fees = 0.0
        if order.side == "SELL":
            sec_fee = exec_notional * self.sec_fee_rate
            finra_fee = min(order.order_size * self.finra_taf_per_share, 8.30)
            reg_fees = sec_fee + finra_fee

        return comm + exchange_fee + reg_fees

    def calculate_implementation_shortfall(
        self, order: OrderContext, market: MarketContext
    ) -> CostBreakdown:
        """
        Calculates total Implementation Shortfall relative to paper benchmark:
        IS = Side * (P_exec - P_0) * Q + Explicit_Costs
        """
        direction = 1.0 if order.side == "BUY" else -1.0
        
        # 1. Dynamic Half-Spread Cost
        dynamic_spread = self.compute_dynamic_spread(order, market)
        half_spread_per_share = dynamic_spread / 2.0

        # 2. Market Impact
        perm_impact_per_share, temp_impact_per_share = self.compute_market_impact(order, market)
        
        # In linear liquidation over duration T, average execution captures 1/2 of permanent impact
        avg_perm_impact = 0.5 * perm_impact_per_share

        # 3. Realized Execution Price
        total_price_drift = half_spread_per_share + avg_perm_impact + temp_impact_per_share
        effective_exec_price = market.arrival_price + (direction * total_price_drift)

        # Dollar component costs
        dollar_spread_cost = half_spread_per_share * order.order_size
        dollar_perm_cost = avg_perm_impact * order.order_size
        dollar_temp_cost = temp_impact_per_share * order.order_size
        
        # 4. Explicit Costs
        exec_notional = effective_exec_price * order.order_size
        explicit_costs = self.compute_explicit_costs(order, exec_notional)

        # 5. Total Implementation Shortfall
        total_shortfall_dollars = (
            dollar_spread_cost + dollar_perm_cost + dollar_temp_cost + explicit_costs
        )
        
        benchmark_notional = market.arrival_price * order.order_size
        total_shortfall_bps = (total_shortfall_dollars / benchmark_notional) * 10_000.0

        return CostBreakdown(
            arrival_price=market.arrival_price,
            effective_price=effective_exec_price,
            spread_cost=dollar_spread_cost,
            permanent_impact=dollar_perm_cost,
            temporary_impact=dollar_temp_cost,
            explicit_costs=explicit_costs,
            total_shortfall_dollars=total_shortfall_dollars,
            total_shortfall_bps=total_shortfall_bps,
            details={
                "dynamic_spread": dynamic_spread,
                "perm_impact_per_share": perm_impact_per_share,
                "temp_impact_per_share": temp_impact_per_share,
                "total_drift_per_share": total_price_drift,
            },
        )

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import yfinance as yf
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Tuple


@dataclass
class BacktestResults:
    sharpe_ratio: float
    sortino_ratio: float
    cagr: float
    max_drawdown: float
    total_trades: int
    win_rate: float
    expected_value_r: float
    equity_curve: pd.Series


class DynamicKalmanStatArb:
    """
    Production Statistical Arbitrage Engine using an Online Kalman Filter
    and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Half-Life dynamic exits on free public data.
    """

    def __init__(
        self,
        asset_y: str = "GDX",
        asset_x: str = "GLD",
        start_date: str = "2018-01-01",
        end_date: str = "2025-01-01",
        entry_z: float = 2.0,
        exit_z: float = 0.25,
        stop_z: float = 3.5,
        risk_per_trade: float = 0.01,   # 1% Account Risk per trade (1R)
        initial_capital: float = 100_000.0,
        cost_bps: float = 5.0           # 5 bps round-trip friction per leg
    ):
        self.asset_y = asset_y
        self.asset_x = asset_x
        self.start_date = start_date
        self.end_date = end_date
        self.entry_z = entry_z
        self.exit_z = exit_z
        self.stop_z = stop_z
        self.risk_per_trade = risk_per_trade
        self.initial_capital = initial_capital
        self.cost_rate = cost_bps / 10_000.0

    def fetch_data(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """Download free daily OHLCV data from Yahoo Finance."""
        tickers = f"{self.asset_y} {self.asset_x}"
        data = yf.download(tickers, start=self.start_date, end=self.end_date, progress=False)["Close"]
        df = data[[self.asset_y, self.asset_x]].dropna()
        df.columns = ["y", "x"]
        return df

    def run_kalman_filter(self, df: pd.DataFrame) -> Tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
        """
        Executes an online Kalman Filter estimating dynamic intercept (alpha)
        and dynamic hedge ratio (beta) on each bar with zero lookahead bias.
        """
        n = len(df)
        y = df["y"].values
        x = df["x"].values

        # State matrix: theta = [alpha, beta]^T
        theta = np.zeros((2, n))
        P = np.zeros((2, 2, n))
        residuals = np.zeros(n)

        # Initial state priors
        theta[:, 0] = [0.0, y[0] / x[0]]
        P[:, :, 0] = np.eye(2) * 1.0

        # System Covariances
        Vw = 1e-4 / (1.0 - 1e-4) * np.eye(2)  # Process noise covariance Q
        Ve = 1e-3                             # Measurement noise covariance R

        for t in range(1, n):
            # 1. State Prediction
            theta_pred = theta[:, t - 1]
            P_pred = P[:, :, t - 1] + Vw

            # 2. Measurement Prediction
            H = np.array([1.0, x[t]])
            y_pred = np.dot(H, theta_pred)
            err = y[t] - y_pred
            residuals[t] = err

            # 3. Kalman Gain Calculation
            S = np.dot(H, np.dot(P_pred, H.T)) + Ve
            K = np.dot(P_pred, H.T) / S

            # 4. State Update
            theta[:, t] = theta_pred + K * err
            P[:, :, t] = P_pred - np.outer(K, np.dot(H, P_pred))

        alphas = theta[0, :]
        betas = theta[1, :]
        return alphas, betas, residuals

    def compute_ou_half_life(self, spread_series: pd.Series, lookback: int = 60) -> float:
        """Calculates Ornstein-Uhlenbeck mean-reversion half-life via AR(1) regression."""
        if len(spread_series) < lookback:
            return 10.0
        s = spread_series.iloc[-lookback:]
        dy = s.diff().dropna()
        y_lag = s.shift(1).dropna()
        
        # OLS fit: dy = theta * (mu - y_lag)
        res = np.polyfit(y_lag.values, dy.values, deg=1)
        theta = -res[0]
        if theta <= 0.001:
            return 40.0  # Default cap if weak mean-reversion
        half_life = np.log(2) / theta
        return max(2.0, min(half_life, 60.0))

    def backtest(self) -> BacktestResults:
        """Executes event-driven simulation with dynamic 1R sizing and exit conditions."""
        df = self.fetch_data()
        alphas, betas, residuals = self.run_kalman_filter(df)
        df["alpha"] = alphas
        df["beta"] = betas
        df["spread"] = residuals

        # Rolling 30-day standardized Z-Score of Kalman residuals
        roll_mean = df["spread"].rolling(window=30).mean()
        roll_std = df["spread"].rolling(window=30).std()
        df["z_score"] = (df["spread"] - roll_mean) / roll_std
        df = df.dropna().copy()

        # Simulation State Tracking
        position = 0          # +1 (Long Spread), -1 (Short Spread), 0 (Flat)
        entry_price_y = 0.0
        entry_price_x = 0.0
        shares_y = 0.0
        shares_x = 0.0
        bars_held = 0
        current_half_life = 10.0
        
        capital = self.initial_capital
        equity_curve = []
        trade_pnl_r = []

        for i in range(len(df)):
            row = df.iloc[i]
            z = row["z_score"]
            py = row["y"]
            px = row["x"]
            b = row["beta"]

            # Mark-to-market portfolio value
            current_portfolio_val = capital
            if position != 0:
                unrealized = (shares_y * (py - entry_price_y)) + (shares_x * (px - entry_price_x))
                current_portfolio_val += unrealized
                bars_held += 1

            # ---------------- EXIT LOGIC ----------------
            if position != 0:
                exit_signal = False
                exit_reason = ""

                # Target Reversion Exit
                if position == 1 and z >= -self.exit_z:
                    exit_signal = True
                    exit_reason = "Mean-Revert"
                elif position == -1 and z <= self.exit_z:
                    exit_signal = True
                    exit_reason = "Mean-Revert"
                
                # Structural Invalidation Stop
                elif abs(z) >= self.stop_z:
                    exit_signal = True
                    exit_reason = "Stop-Loss"
                
                # Half-Life Time Expiration Exit
                elif bars_held > int(2.0 * current_half_life):
                    exit_signal = True
                    exit_reason = "Time-Stop"

                if exit_signal:
                    gross_pnl = (shares_y * (py - entry_price_y)) + (shares_x * (px - entry_price_x))
                    exit_cost = (abs(shares_y * py) + abs(shares_x * px)) * self.cost_rate
                    net_pnl = gross_pnl - exit_cost
                    
                    capital += net_pnl
                    dollar_risk = capital * self.risk_per_trade
                    realized_r = net_pnl / max(dollar_risk, 1.0)
                    trade_pnl_r.append(realized_r)

                    # Reset State
                    position = 0
                    shares_y = 0.0
                    shares_x = 0.0
                    bars_held = 0

            # ---------------- ENTRY LOGIC ----------------
            if position == 0:
                if z <= -self.entry_z:     # Oversold Spread -> Long y, Short x
                    position = 1
                elif z >= self.entry_z:    # Overbought Spread -> Short y, Long x
                    position = -1

                if position != 0:
                    current_half_life = self.compute_ou_half_life(df["spread"].iloc[:i+1])
                    dollar_risk = capital * self.risk_per_trade  # Standardized 1R
                    
                    # Size based on spread volatility risk
                    spread_std = roll_std.iloc[i]
                    dollar_allocation = dollar_risk / max(spread_std, 1e-4)
                    
                    # Hedge-ratio balanced unit allocation
                    target_notional_y = min(dollar_allocation, capital * 0.5)
                    shares_y = position * (target_notional_y / py)
                    shares_x = -position * b * (target_notional_y / px)

                    entry_price_y = py
                    entry_price_x = px
                    
                    # Deduct entry friction
                    entry_cost = (abs(shares_y * py) + abs(shares_x * px)) * self.cost_rate
                    capital -= entry_cost
                    bars_held = 0

            equity_curve.append(current_portfolio_val)

        # Performance Metric Calculations
        eq = pd.Series(equity_curve, index=df.index)
        daily_returns = eq.pct_change().dropna()
        
        years = len(df) / 252.0
        cagr = (eq.iloc[-1] / eq.iloc[0]) ** (1.0 / years) - 1.0
        sharpe = (daily_returns.mean() / (daily_returns.std() + 1e-6)) * np.sqrt(252)
        
        downside = daily_returns[daily_returns < 0]
        sortino = (daily_returns.mean() / (downside.std() + 1e-6)) * np.sqrt(252)
        
        cum_max = eq.cummax()
        drawdown = (eq - cum_max) / cum_max
        max_dd = drawdown.min()

        pnl_arr = np.array(trade_pnl_r)
        win_rate = np.mean(pnl_arr > 0) if len(pnl_arr) > 0 else 0.0
        ev_r = np.mean(pnl_arr) if len(pnl_arr) > 0 else 0.0

        return BacktestResults(
            sharpe_ratio=sharpe,
            sortino_ratio=sortino,
            cagr=cagr,
            max_drawdown=max_dd,
            total_trades=len(trade_pnl_r),
            win_rate=win_rate,
            expected_value_r=ev_r,
            equity_curve=eq
        )

Part 4: Risk Management & Circuit Breakers
To ensure account survival and uninterrupted compounding, institutional quant models enforce non-negotiable risk boundaries above all alpha signals:
 * Portfolio Heat Ceiling: Aggregate risk exposure across all active positions must not exceed 3.0R to 4.0R of account equity.
 * Leverage Constraints: Total gross leverage is capped at \le 2.0\times for equities and \le 4.0\times for futures/FX to prevent margin calls during tail-risk events.
 * The 3-Tier Circuit Breaker:
   * Intraday Gate (-3R): Liquidate active day positions and pause new entries for 24 hours.
   * Rolling Drawdown Step-Down (-10\% Equity): Automatically scale all position sizing down by 50\% across all sleeves until positive expectancy resumes.
   * Hard Model Decommission Gate: If the rolling 60-day Sharpe ratio drops below 0.50 or drawdown duration exceeds 1.5\times the historical maximum losing streak (L_{\text{max}}), take the model offline for recalibration.
