Raw data is a commodity, but raw data is not alpha. Alpha is generated through the mathematical transformations applied to that data, the ability to exploit non-economic counterparty flows, and how efficiently trades are executed after accounting for transaction friction.

1. How Alpha is Generated from the Same Data
When two quant funds look at the exact same public data (e.g., tick history, 10-Year TIPS yields, or satellite tiles), their returns diverge drastically based on three operational layers:

[Identical Public Data] 
       │
       ├──► Feature Engineering (Non-linear extraction, transformations, noise filtering)
       ├──► Signal Fusion (Orthogonalizing weak predictors into a strong meta-model)
       └──► Execution Optimization (Minimizing market impact, slippage, and fees)
               │
               ▼
       [Divergent Alpha & Profitability]
Feature Engineering & Representation: Two quants download the same Sentinel-1 SAR satellite radar data. A naive trader calculates mean pixel brightness. A sophisticated quant trains a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with a shadow-geometry algorithm to calculate precise tank-roof displacement in cubic meters.

Noise Filtering & Signal Extraction: Market data has an extremely low Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR < 5%). Superior models use advanced filtering (such as Kalman Filters, Wavelet transforms, or Fractional Differentiation) to preserve memory while making time-series stationary, whereas basic indicators (like RSI or Moving Averages) introduce lag and false breakouts.

Orthogonality & Signal Fusion: Individual signals rarely have an Information Coefficient (IC) higher than 0.03 to 0.08. Top quantitative models combine dozens of uncorrelated (orthogonal) weak signals using ensemble techniques, PCA, or cross-sectional ranking.

2. Where Profitability Actually Comes From
Quant trading is a zero-sum game net of transaction costs. Profitability does not appear out of thin air; it is extracted directly from market participants trading for non-alpha reasons:

Counterparty	Why They Trade (Their Incentive)	How Quants Profit From Them
Commercial Hedgers	Airlines hedging jet fuel; multinational corporations hedging foreign currency exposure.	Liquidity & Insurance Premium: Hedgers accept unfavorable market pricing to eliminate balance-sheet variance; quants absorb the risk and capture the spread/carry.
Retail Noise Traders	Emotion-driven retail traders entering on laggy technical indicators or FOMO.	Adverse Selection & Mean Reversion: Quants fade overextended retail retail liquidity clusters, stop-loss cascades, and structural behavioral biases.
Mandate-Constrained Institutional Funds	Passive index funds, mutual funds, and pension funds required to rebalance or track benchmarks at fixed dates/times.	Front-Running Predictable Flow: Quants anticipate forced index additions/deletions, quarterly rebalancing, and month-end portfolio reallocations.
Central Banks	Intervening to defend currency pegs or stabilize domestic bond yields (not profit-seeking).	Macro Dislocation Trading: Exploiting market distortions and pricing anomalies created by artificial policy interventions.
3. What Determines Superior Model Performance
A common misconception is that model performance comes down to using more complex machine learning models (like deep neural networks). In practice, complex models often overfit financial noise. High-performing quant models excel across four structural metrics:

1. The Fundamental Law of Active Management
The expected performance (Information Ratio) of a strategy is governed by Grinold and Kahn's Fundamental Law:

IR≈IC× 
Breadth

​
 
IC (Information Coefficient): The correlation between the model's forecast and actual future returns.

Breadth: The number of independent trading decisions made per year.

Implication: A model does not need a high win rate (e.g., an IC=0.05, or a 52.5% win rate, is elite). Applying that minor edge across 10,000 independent trades across FX, commodities, and equities generates a high Sharpe ratio through law of large numbers.

2. Out-of-Sample Robustness vs. Overfitting

Poor models over-optimize parameters (e.g., finding the "best" period for an EMA on historical backtests), resulting in catastrophic live failure.

Superior models use Purged & Embargoed Cross-Validation (to prevent data leakage in time-series data) and calculate the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) to adjust for selection bias and backtest trial counts.

3. Execution Efficiency & Implementation Shortfall

A strategy with a theoretical backtested Sharpe Ratio of 3.0 can have a realized Sharpe Ratio of 0.5 if it enters via market orders with poor fill logic.

High-performing systems model Transaction Cost Analysis (TCA) directly inside the optimization objective, using limit order placement, Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) execution, and dark pool routing to avoid paying the bid-ask spread and market impact costs.

4. Dynamic Regime Awareness
Static models fail when the macroeconomic backdrop changes. High-performing architectures implement Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) or Volatility Regimes to dynamically scale leverage, reduce risk during high-volatility structural shifts, or turn off mean-reversion engines when trends break out.

Explain how Purged and Embargoed Cross-Validation works for financial time series to prevent data leakage.

Standard K-Fold cross-validation assumes data is Independent and Identically Distributed (i.i.d.). import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from scipy.stats import norm, skew, kurtosis

# =======================================================================
# 1. Rolling Information Coefficient (IC)
# =======================================================================
def rolling_information_coefficient(
    signals: pd.DataFrame, 
    forward_returns: pd.DataFrame, 
    window: int = 60, 
    method: str = "spearman"
) -> pd.Series:
    """
    Computes rolling cross-sectional Information Coefficient across multi-asset panels.
    
    Parameters:
    - signals: DataFrame (Date x Asset) of model alpha predictions
    - forward_returns: DataFrame (Date x Asset) of realized forward returns
    - window: Rolling window length in periods
    - method: 'spearman' (rank correlation) or 'pearson'
    
    Returns:
    - pd.Series of rolling average IC
    """
    # Cross-sectional correlation per timestamp
    daily_ic = signals.corrwith(forward_returns, axis=1, method=method)
    rolling_ic = daily_ic.rolling(window=window, min_periods=max(5, window // 2)).mean()
    return rolling_ic


# =======================================================================
# 2. Information Ratio (IR)
# =======================================================================
def calculate_information_ratio(
    strategy_returns: pd.Series, 
    benchmark_returns: pd.Series = None, 
    periods_per_year: int = 252
) -> float:
    """
    Computes Annualized Information Ratio. 
    If benchmark is None, calculates pure Strategy Sharpe/IR against 0 risk-free rate.
    """
    if benchmark_returns is not None:
        active_return = strategy_returns - benchmark_returns
    else:
        active_return = strategy_returns

    mean_active = active_return.mean()
    std_active = active_return.std(ddof=1)
    
    if std_active == 0 or np.isnan(std_active):
        return 0.0
        
    return (mean_active / std_active) * np.sqrt(periods_per_year)


def calculate_ic_information_ratio(ic_series: pd.Series, periods_per_year: int = 252) -> float:
    """Computes IC Information Ratio (Signal stability metric)."""
    clean_ic = ic_series.dropna()
    if len(clean_ic) < 2 or clean_ic.std() == 0:
        return 0.0
    return (clean_ic.mean() / clean_ic.std(ddof=1)) * np.sqrt(periods_per_year)


# =======================================================================
# 3. Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)
# =======================================================================
def expected_maximum_sharpe(n_trials: int, var_sharpe: float = 1.0) -> float:
    """
    Approximates expected maximum Sharpe ratio under the null hypothesis (H0: SR = 0)
    using extreme value theory and Euler-Mascheroni expansion.
    """
    if n_trials <= 1:
        return 0.0
    
    gamma_e = 0.57721566490153286  # Euler-Mascheroni constant
    e = np.exp(1.0)
    
    z_1 = norm.ppf(1.0 - 1.0 / n_trials)
    z_2 = norm.ppf(1.0 - 1.0 / (n_trials * e))
    
    sr_star = np.sqrt(var_sharpe) * ((1.0 - gamma_e) * z_1 + gamma_e * z_2)
    return sr_star


def deflated_sharpe_ratio(
    returns: pd.Series, 
    n_trials: int = 1, 
    var_sharpe_trials: float = None,
    periods_per_year: int = 252
) -> dict:
    """
    Calculates Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) and Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (PSR).
    
    Parameters:
    - returns: Series of periodic strategy returns (unannualized)
    - n_trials: Number of strategy configurations / backtests tested
    - var_sharpe_trials: Variance of Sharpe ratios across all tested trials (optional)
    - periods_per_year: 252 for daily, 52 for weekly, 12 for monthly
    
    Returns:
    - dict containing estimated SR, annualized SR, DSR p-value, and expected max SR.
    """
    clean_returns = returns.dropna().values
    t = len(clean_returns)
    if t < 5:
        raise ValueError("Insufficient sample size for DSR estimation.")
    
    # 1. Periodic (unannualized) moments
    mean = np.mean(clean_returns)
    std = np.std(clean_returns, ddof=1)
    sr_hat = mean / std if std > 0 else 0.0
    
    # Skewness and Pearson Kurtosis (Fisher=False -> Normal = 3)
    gamma_3 = skew(clean_returns)
    gamma_4 = kurtosis(clean_returns, fisher=False)
    
    # 2. Variance of the estimated periodic Sharpe ratio under non-normality
    sr_std_err = np.sqrt((1.0 - gamma_3 * sr_hat + ((gamma_4 - 1.0) / 4.0) * (sr_hat ** 2)) / (t - 1.0))
    
    # 3. Benchmark SR threshold calculation
    if var_sharpe_trials is None:
        # Default to sampling variance if cross-trial variance is unknown
        var_sharpe = 1.0 / (t - 1.0)
    else:
        # Convert annualized trial variance to periodic variance
        var_sharpe = var_sharpe_trials / periods_per_year
        
    sr_benchmark = expected_maximum_sharpe(n_trials=n_trials, var_sharpe=var_sharpe)
    
    # 4. Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio & Deflated Sharpe Ratio
    # PSR checks probability against 0; DSR checks probability against expected max SR*
    z_dsr = (sr_hat - sr_benchmark) / sr_std_err
    dsr_stat = norm.cdf(z_dsr)
    
    return {
        "Annualized_SR": sr_hat * np.sqrt(periods_per_year),
        "Periodic_SR": sr_hat,
        "Expected_Max_SR_Periodic": sr_benchmark,
        "Expected_Max_SR_Annualized": sr_benchmark * np.sqrt(periods_per_year),
        "Skewness": gamma_3,
        "Kurtosis": gamma_4,
        "DSR": dsr_stat,  # Probability that true SR > 0 after adjusting for N trials
        "Track_Record_Periods": t
    }

import itertools
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from scipy.stats import norm, rankdata, skew, kurtosis

def compute_sharpe(returns: np.ndarray, periods_per_year: int = 252) -> float:
    """Calculates annualized Sharpe Ratio."""
    std = np.std(returns, ddof=1)
    if std == 0 or np.isnan(std):
        return 0.0
    return (np.mean(returns) / std) * np.sqrt(periods_per_year)

def calculate_pbo_and_dsr(
    pnl_matrix: pd.DataFrame, 
    n_blocks: int = 10, 
    k_test_blocks: int = 5,
    periods_per_year: int = 252
) -> dict:
    """
    Computes PBO and DSR from CPCV backtest trials.
    
    Parameters:
    - pnl_matrix: DataFrame of shape (T timestamps, M strategy configurations)
    - n_blocks: Total number of time blocks for partitioning (S)
    - k_test_blocks: Number of blocks assigned to out-of-sample testing
    - periods_per_year: 252 for daily data
    
    Returns:
    - dict with PBO, DSR, and distribution metrics.
    """
    t_samples, m_trials = pnl_matrix.shape
    returns_arr = pnl_matrix.values
    
    # 1. Split time-series into S contiguous blocks
    block_indices = np.array_split(np.arange(t_samples), n_blocks)
    
    # 2. Generate all combinations of test blocks: C(n_blocks, k_test_blocks)
    combos = list(itertools.combinations(range(n_blocks), k_test_blocks))
    n_combos = len(combos)
    
    omega_list = []
    logits_list = []
    
    # 3. Iterate through CPCV combinations
    for test_block_ids in combos:
        train_block_ids = [b for b in range(n_blocks) if b not in test_block_ids]
        
        # Flatten sample indices
        test_idx = np.concatenate([block_indices[b] for b in test_block_ids])
        train_idx = np.concatenate([block_indices[b] for b in train_block_ids])
        
        train_returns = returns_arr[train_idx, :]
        test_returns = returns_arr[test_idx, :]
        
        # In-sample & Out-of-sample Sharpe ratios for all M models
        is_sharpes = np.array([compute_sharpe(train_returns[:, m]) for m in range(m_trials)])
        oos_sharpes = np.array([compute_sharpe(test_returns[:, m]) for m in range(m_trials)])
        
        # Best model in-sample
        best_is_model = np.argmax(is_sharpes)
        
        # Rank of the best IS model in OOS (1 = worst, M = best)
        oos_ranks = rankdata(oos_sharpes)
        best_oos_rank = oos_ranks[best_is_model]
        
        # Relative OOS percentile
        omega = best_oos_rank / (m_trials + 1.0)
        omega_list.append(omega)
        
        # Logit metric
        logit = np.log(omega / (1.0 - omega))
        logits_list.append(logit)
        
    omega_arr = np.array(omega_list)
    
    # 4. Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)
    pbo = np.mean(omega_arr <= 0.5)
    
    # 5. Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) Calculation
    # Aggregate OOS performance for all trials
    overall_trial_sharpes = np.array([compute_sharpe(returns_arr[:, m], periods_per_year) for m in range(m_trials)])
    best_overall_model = np.argmax(overall_trial_sharpes)
    
    best_series = returns_arr[:, best_overall_model]
    best_sr_unann = np.mean(best_series) / np.std(best_series, ddof=1)
    best_sr_ann = best_sr_unann * np.sqrt(periods_per_year)
    
    # Non-normality moments
    gamma_3 = skew(best_series)
    gamma_4 = kurtosis(best_series, fisher=False)
    
    # Variance across all M trials (periodic)
    var_sharpe_periodic = np.var(overall_trial_sharpes / np.sqrt(periods_per_year), ddof=1)
    
    # Expected maximum Sharpe threshold
    gamma_e = 0.57721566490153286
    z1 = norm.ppf(1.0 - 1.0 / m_trials)
    z2 = norm.ppf(1.0 - 1.0 / (m_trials * np.e))
    sr_star_periodic = np.sqrt(var_sharpe_periodic) * ((1.0 - gamma_e) * z1 + gamma_e * z2)
    sr_star_ann = sr_star_periodic * np.sqrt(periods_per_year)
    
    # DSR calculation
    sr_std_err = np.sqrt((1.0 - gamma_3 * best_sr_unann + ((gamma_4 - 1.0) / 4.0) * (best_sr_unann ** 2)) / (t_samples - 1.0))
    z_dsr = (best_sr_unann - sr_star_periodic) / sr_std_err
    dsr = norm.cdf(z_dsr)
    
    return {
        "PBO": pbo,
        "Mean_OOS_Percentile": np.mean(omega_arr),
        "Best_Model_Index": int(best_overall_model),
        "Observed_Sharpe_Annualized": best_sr_ann,
        "Expected_Max_Null_Sharpe": sr_star_ann,
        "Deflated_Sharpe_Ratio": dsr,
        "Total_CPCV_Splits": n_combos
    }

import cv2
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def create_synthetic_tank_image() -> np.ndarray:
    """Generates a synthetic satellite crop of a floating-roof oil tank."""
    img = np.full((500, 500), 120, dtype=np.uint8)  # Ground background
    center = (250, 250)
    radius = 150

    # Draw tank rim (outer shell)
    cv2.circle(img, center, radius + 4, 220, -1)
    # Draw floating roof base
    cv2.circle(img, center, radius, 175, -1)

    # Simulate inner wall crescent shadow (sun from top-left, 45 deg)
    shadow_mask = np.zeros((500, 500), dtype=np.uint8)
    # Tank boundary
    cv2.circle(shadow_mask, center, radius, 255, -1)
    
    # Offset roof illuminated area (shifted down-right by shadow displacement)
    sun_offset = (250 + 45, 250 + 45)  # 45px shadow displacement
    sun_mask = np.zeros((500, 500), dtype=np.uint8)
    cv2.circle(sun_mask, sun_offset, radius, 255, -1)

    # Crescent shadow = (Tank Interior) AND NOT (Sunlit Region)
    inner_shadow = cv2.bitwise_and(shadow_mask, cv2.bitwise_not(sun_mask))
    img[inner_shadow > 0] = 35  # Dark shadow value

    # Add Gaussian sensor noise
    noise = np.random.normal(0, 4, img.shape).astype(np.int16)
    noisy_img = np.clip(img.astype(np.int16) + noise, 0, 255).astype(np.uint8)
    return noisy_img


def analyze_floating_roof_tank(
    image: np.ndarray, 
    sun_elevation_deg: float = 45.0, 
    tank_height_m: float = 20.0,
    m_per_pixel: float = 0.5
) -> dict:
    """
    Detects tank perimeter and measures inner shadow area & fill level.
    
    Parameters:
    - image: Grayscale image (uint8)
    - sun_elevation_deg: Sun/radar elevation angle in degrees
    - tank_height_m: Known physical height of tank in meters
    - m_per_pixel: Ground Sample Distance (GSD) resolution
    """
    # 1. Preprocessing & Smoothing
    blurred = cv2.GaussianBlur(image, (7, 7), 1.5)

    # 2. Detect Circular Tank Boundary using Hough Transform
    circles = cv2.HoughCircles(
        blurred,
        cv2.HOUGH_GRADIENT,
        dp=1.2,
        minDist=100,
        param1=50,
        param2=30,
        minRadius=80,
        maxRadius=200
    )

    if circles is None:
        raise ValueError("No circular tank detected in image.")

    circles = np.uint16(np.around(circles))
    cx, cy, r = circles[0][0]

    # 3. Create Region of Interest (ROI) mask for tank interior
    tank_mask = np.zeros_like(image)
    cv2.circle(tank_mask, (cx, cy), r - 3, 255, -1)  # inset 3px to exclude wall rim
    total_tank_pixels = np.sum(tank_mask > 0)

    # 4. Shadow Segmentation (Otsu adaptive thresholding inside tank)
    tank_pixels = image[tank_mask > 0]
    thresh_val, _ = cv2.threshold(tank_pixels, 0, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY_INV + cv2.THRESH_OTSU)
    
    _, binary_shadow = cv2.threshold(image, thresh_val, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY_INV)
    inner_shadow = cv2.bitwise_and(binary_shadow, binary_shadow, mask=tank_mask)

    # Morphological cleaning to remove salt-and-pepper noise
    kernel = cv2.getStructuringElement(cv2.MORPH_ELLIPSE, (3, 3))
    inner_shadow = cv2.morphologyEx(inner_shadow, cv2.MORPH_OPEN, kernel)

    # 5. Measure Metrics
    shadow_pixels = np.sum(inner_shadow > 0)
    shadow_ratio = shadow_pixels / total_tank_pixels

    # Estimate physical height of liquid:
    # Max theoretical shadow ratio for 100% empty tank at this angle
    theta_rad = np.radians(sun_elevation_deg)
    max_shadow_length_px = (tank_height_m * (1.0 / np.tan(theta_rad))) / m_per_pixel
    max_shadow_ratio_approx = min(1.0, (max_shadow_length_px / (2 * r)))

    estimated_fill_pct = np.clip((1.0 - (shadow_ratio / max_shadow_ratio_approx)) * 100.0, 0.0, 100.0)

    # 6. Generate Diagnostic Visualization
    overlay = cv2.cvtColor(image, cv2.COLOR_GRAY2BGR)
    # Draw detected tank boundary in green
    cv2.circle(overlay, (cx, cy), r, (0, 255, 0), 2)
    # Highlight shadow in red
    overlay[inner_shadow > 0] = [0, 0, 255]

    return {
        "center": (int(cx), int(cy)),
        "radius_px": int(r),
        "total_tank_area_px": int(total_tank_pixels),
        "shadow_area_px": int(shadow_pixels),
        "shadow_area_ratio": float(shadow_ratio),
        "estimated_fill_percent": float(estimated_fill_pct),
        "visual_overlay": overlay
    }

How do you set up a deep learning object detection pipeline (YOLOv8/Mask R-CNN) to detect floating-roof tanks automatically across large satellite scenes?

Processing large satellite scenes (often 10,000×10,000+ pixels in multi-gigabyte GeoTIFFs) requires a specialized computer vision workflow. Standard deep learning architectures cannot digest gigapixel images directly without downsampling, which destroys the fine spatial resolution needed to detect tank rims and shadows.

The solution is an Instance Segmentation Pipeline (YOLOv8-seg / Mask R-CNN) coupled with Slicing Aided Hyper Inference (SAHI) and geospatial coordinate reprojection.

Step 3: End-to-End Gigapixel Inference Pipeline
This script slices a large satellite GeoTIFF, runs batch instance segmentation, stitches overlapping detections using boundary-aware Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS), and maps the results to geospatial coordinates.

Python
import rasterio
from rasterio.windows import Window
import numpy as np
import cv2
import torch
import torchvision
from ultralytics import YOLO
from shapely.geometry import Polygon, box
import geopandas as gpd

class SatelliteTankPipeline:
    def __init__(self, model_path: str, tile_size: int = 640, overlap: float = 0.25):
        self.model = YOLO(model_path)
        self.tile_size = tile_size
        self.stride = int(tile_size * (1.0 - overlap))
        self.overlap = overlap

    def process_large_scene(self, geotiff_path: str, conf_thresh: float = 0.4, iou_thresh: float = 0.4):
        with rasterio.open(geotiff_path) as src:
            width, height = src.width, src.height
            transform = src.transform
            crs = src.crs
            
            # Read RGB bands (or 1-band SAR scaled to 3-channel uint8)
            img_data = src.read([1, 2, 3])
            # Transpose (C, H, W) -> (H, W, C)
            full_img = np.transpose(img_data, (1, 2, 0))
            if full_img.dtype != np.uint8:
                full_img = cv2.normalize(full_img, None, 0, 255, cv2.NORM_MINMAX).astype(np.uint8)

        global_boxes = []
        global_scores = []
        global_masks = []
        global_classes = []

        # 1. Sliding Window Tiling
        for y in range(0, height, self.stride):
            for x in range(0, width, self.stride):
                # Ensure window does not exceed image bounds
                w = min(self.tile_size, width - x)
                h = min(self.tile_size, height - y)
                tile = full_img[y:y+h, x:x+w]

                # Pad tile to square if near boundaries
                if h != self.tile_size or w != self.tile_size:
                    padded_tile = np.zeros((self.tile_size, self.tile_size, 3), dtype=np.uint8)
                    padded_tile[:h, :w] = tile
                    tile = padded_tile

                # 2. Run Inference on Tile
                results = self.model.predict(tile, conf=conf_thresh, verbose=False)[0]

                if results.boxes is not None and len(results.boxes) > 0:
                    boxes = results.boxes.xyxy.cpu().numpy()
                    scores = results.boxes.conf.cpu().numpy()
                    classes = results.boxes.cls.cpu().numpy()
                    
                    # Offset local coordinates to global image pixel coordinates
                    for idx in range(len(boxes)):
                        gx1, gy1, gx2, gy2 = boxes[idx]
                        global_boxes.append([gx1 + x, gy1 + y, gx2 + x, gy2 + y])
                        global_scores.append(scores[idx])
                        global_classes.append(classes[idx])

        if not global_boxes:
            return gpd.GeoDataFrame()

        # 3. Global Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) across tile boundaries
        boxes_t = torch.tensor(global_boxes, dtype=torch.float32)
        scores_t = torch.tensor(global_scores, dtype=torch.float32)
        keep_indices = torchvision.ops.nms(boxes_t, scores_t, iou_thresh).numpy()

        # 4. Filter Detections & Reproject to Geo-Coordinates
        geometries = []
        final_scores = []
        final_classes = []
        
        for idx in keep_indices:
            x1, y1, x2, y2 = global_boxes[idx]
            # Convert pixel bounding box to geographic coordinates
            geo_x1, geo_y1 = rasterio.transform.xy(transform, y1, x1)
            geo_x2, geo_y2 = rasterio.transform.xy(transform, y2, x2)
            
            geo_poly = box(min(geo_x1, geo_x2), min(geo_y1, geo_y2), 
                           max(geo_x1, geo_x2), max(geo_y1, geo_y2))
            geometries.append(geo_poly)
            final_scores.append(global_scores[idx])
            final_classes.append("tank" if global_classes[idx] == 0 else "shadow")

        # 5. Build GeoDataFrame
        gdf = gpd.GeoDataFrame({
            "class": final_classes,
            "confidence": final_scores,
            "geometry": geometries
        }, crs=crs)

        return gdf
Step 4: Measuring Storage Volumes Across Detected Tanks
Once tanks and shadows are extracted into global vectors, compute the spatial intersection to pair each shadow with its corresponding tank:

def compute_tank_fill_rates(gdf_results: gpd.GeoDataFrame) -> gpd.GeoDataFrame:
    """Calculates fill level percentages by intersecting tank and shadow geometries."""
    tanks = gdf_results[gdf_results["class"] == "tank"].copy()
    shadows = gdf_results[gdf_results["class"] == "shadow"].copy()

    fill_levels = []

    for _, tank in tanks.iterrows():
        tank_geom = tank.geometry
        tank_area = tank_geom.area

        # Find shadows intersecting this specific tank
        overlapping_shadows = shadows[shadows.geometry.intersects(tank_geom)]
        
        if not overlapping_shadows.empty:
            # Union overlapping shadow areas inside the tank
            shadow_geom = overlapping_shadows.geometry.unary_union.intersection(tank_geom)
            shadow_area = shadow_geom.area
            shadow_ratio = shadow_area / tank_area
            
            # Assuming max observable shadow ratio is ~0.45 under typical sun angles
            estimated_fill = np.clip((1.0 - (shadow_ratio / 0.45)) * 100.0, 0.0, 100.0)
        else:
            # No shadow detected = tank is 100% full (roof at the top rim)
            estimated_fill = 100.0

        fill_levels.append(estimated_fill)

    tanks["estimated_fill_percent"] = fill_levels
    return tanks

Python Implementation with cv2.fitEllipse
This self-contained script creates a synthetic oblique satellite view of a floating-roof oil tank, fits an oriented ellipse to the tank rim, segments the inner shadow, and computes fill metrics.

Python
import cv2
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def generate_synthetic_oblique_tank(
    off_nadir_deg: float = 30.0, 
    rotation_deg: float = 35.0, 
    fill_ratio: float = 0.65
) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Generates a synthetic oblique satellite crop of a floating-roof tank.
    """
    img = np.full((600, 600), 125, dtype=np.uint8)  # Terrain background
    center = (300, 300)
    radius_major = 160
    radius_minor = int(radius_major * np.cos(np.radians(off_nadir_deg)))
    axes = (radius_major, radius_minor)

    # 1. Outer rim of the tank
    cv2.ellipse(img, center, (axes[0] + 3, axes[1] + 3), rotation_deg, 0, 360, 220, -1)
    
    # 2. Base floating roof
    cv2.ellipse(img, center, axes, rotation_deg, 0, 360, 170, -1)

    # 3. Inner crescent shadow cast on floating roof
    shadow_displacement = int((1.0 - fill_ratio) * 60)
    offset_rad = np.radians(rotation_deg + 45)  # Shadow offset vector
    offset_center = (
        int(center[0] + shadow_displacement * np.cos(offset_rad)),
        int(center[1] + shadow_displacement * np.sin(offset_rad))
    )

    tank_mask = np.zeros((600, 600), dtype=np.uint8)
    cv2.ellipse(tank_mask, center, axes, rotation_deg, 0, 360, 255, -1)

    sunlit_mask = np.zeros((600, 600), dtype=np.uint8)
    cv2.ellipse(sunlit_mask, offset_center, axes, rotation_deg, 0, 360, 255, -1)

    crescent_shadow = cv2.bitwise_and(tank_mask, cv2.bitwise_not(sunlit_mask))
    img[crescent_shadow > 0] = 30  # Dark shadow pixels

    # Add Gaussian sensor noise
    noise = np.random.normal(0, 3, img.shape).astype(np.int16)
    noisy_img = np.clip(img.astype(np.int16) + noise, 0, 255).astype(np.uint8)
    return noisy_img


def analyze_oblique_floating_tank(
    image: np.ndarray, 
    sun_elevation_deg: float = 40.0, 
    tank_height_m: float = 22.0,
    m_per_pixel: float = 0.5
) -> dict:
    """
    Fits an oriented ellipse to the tank rim and segments the shadow area.
    """
    # 1. Noise reduction and edge detection
    blurred = cv2.GaussianBlur(image, (5, 5), 1.2)
    edges = cv2.Canny(blurred, threshold1=40, threshold2=120)

    # 2. Extract structural contours
    contours, _ = cv2.findContours(edges, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_NONE)
    if not contours:
        raise ValueError("No valid contours found in image.")

    # 3. Filter candidates and fit ellipse
    best_ellipse = None
    max_area = 0
    
    for cnt in contours:
        # cv2.fitEllipse requires at least 5 points
        if len(cnt) >= 5:
            area = cv2.contourArea(cnt)
            if area > 10000:  # Minimum tank area threshold
                ellipse_candidate = cv2.fitEllipse(cnt)
                # Ensure candidate is reasonably circular/elliptical, not an irregular polygon
                (cx, cy), (major_axis, minor_axis), angle = ellipse_candidate
                if minor_axis > 0:
                    aspect_ratio = major_axis / minor_axis
                    if 1.0 <= aspect_ratio <= 3.0 and area > max_area:
                        max_area = area
                        best_ellipse = ellipse_candidate

    if best_ellipse is None:
        raise ValueError("Failed to fit an ellipse to tank perimeter.")

    (cx, cy), (axis_2a, axis_2b), rotation_angle = best_ellipse
    semi_major = axis_2a / 2.0
    semi_minor = axis_2b / 2.0

    # 4. Generate elliptical ROI mask (inset by 3px to eliminate wall rim effects)
    tank_mask = np.zeros_like(image)
    inset_axes = (int(semi_major - 3), int(semi_minor - 3))
    cv2.ellipse(tank_mask, (int(cx), int(cy)), inset_axes, rotation_angle, 0, 360, 255, -1)
    total_tank_pixels = np.sum(tank_mask > 0)

    # 5. Segment inner shadow using Otsu thresholding inside the ellipse
    tank_pixels = image[tank_mask > 0]
    thresh_val, _ = cv2.threshold(tank_pixels, 0, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY_INV + cv2.THRESH_OTSU)
    
    _, binary_shadow = cv2.threshold(image, thresh_val, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY_INV)
    inner_shadow = cv2.bitwise_and(binary_shadow, binary_shadow, mask=tank_mask)

    # Morphological cleaning
    kernel = cv2.getStructuringElement(cv2.MORPH_ELLIPSE, (3, 3))
    inner_shadow = cv2.morphologyEx(inner_shadow, cv2.MORPH_OPEN, kernel)

    # 6. Area and fill estimation
    shadow_pixels = np.sum(inner_shadow > 0)
    shadow_ratio = shadow_pixels / total_tank_pixels

    # Inferred off-nadir viewing angle from ellipse eccentricity
    estimated_off_nadir_rad = np.arccos(np.clip(semi_minor / semi_major, 0.0, 1.0))
    estimated_off_nadir_deg = np.degrees(estimated_off_nadir_rad)

    # Calculate theoretical maximum shadow ratio at this geometry
    theta_rad = np.radians(sun_elevation_deg)
    max_shadow_len_px = (tank_height_m * (1.0 / np.tan(theta_rad))) / m_per_pixel
    max_shadow_ratio_baseline = min(1.0, max_shadow_len_px / (2.0 * semi_major))
    
    estimated_fill_pct = np.clip((1.0 - (shadow_ratio / max_shadow_ratio_baseline)) * 100.0, 0.0, 100.0)

    # 7. Diagnostic visualization overlay
    overlay = cv2.cvtColor(image, cv2.COLOR_GRAY2BGR)
    # Draw fitted outer ellipse in green
    cv2.ellipse(overlay, best_ellipse, (0, 255, 0), 2)
    # Highlight segmented shadow in red
    overlay[inner_shadow > 0] = [0, 0, 255]

    return {
        "center": (round(cx, 1), round(cy, 1)),
        "semi_major_axis_px": round(semi_major, 1),
        "semi_minor_axis_px": round(semi_minor, 1),
        "ellipse_rotation_deg": round(rotation_angle, 1),
        "estimated_off_nadir_angle": round(estimated_off_nadir_deg, 1),
        "shadow_area_ratio": float(shadow_ratio),
        "estimated_fill_percent": float(estimated_fill_pct),
        "overlay": overlay
    }
