# Principal Portfolios: Cross-Predictability and Asset Pricing

**Source**: Kelly et al., Journal of Finance; NBER 27388

## Overview
Novel asset pricing framework using prediction matrices and singular value decomposition.

## Core Concepts

### Prediction Matrix
```
Π = E(R_{t+1} S_t')
```
- Captures own-signal predictability (diagonal)
- Captures cross-predictability (off-diagonal)
- All securities' signals predict each individual return

### Symmetric/Antisymmetric Decomposition
```
Π = Π^s + Π^a
where:
- Π^s = (Π + Π')/2 (symmetric)
- Π^a = (Π - Π')/2 (antisymmetric)
```

### Principal Portfolios (PPs)
- Singular vectors of prediction matrix
- Ordered from most to least predictable
- Most "timeable" portfolios

### Principal Exposure Portfolios (PEPs)
- Eigenvectors of symmetric component Π^s
- Positive factor exposure
- Nonnegative average returns in arbitrage-free markets

### Principal Alpha Portfolios (PAPs)
- Eigenvectors of antisymmetric component Π^a
- Zero factor exposure
- Can deliver positive expected returns = pure alpha!

## Key Results

### Alpha-Beta Decomposition
- Symmetric part determines risk (beta)
- Antisymmetric part enables alpha
- Factor-neutral strategies from antisymmetric eigenvectors

### Asset Pricing Test
- Correctly specified pricing kernel → Π symmetric and positive definite
- No alpha portfolios should exist
- Negative eigenvalues of Π^s = strategies with negative factor exposure and positive returns

### Empirical Performance
- PPs deliver significant out-of-sample alphas
- PEPs have significant factor exposure
- PAPs have little-to-no factor exposure
- Robust across international equities and futures

## Implications for Quant Trading
1. Cross-predictability contains valuable information
2. Antisymmetric strategies offer pure alpha
3. Prediction matrix eigenvalues order strategy quality
4. Framework applicable to any signal-based trading
